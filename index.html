How a Living Lab project works

  1. Choose or propose

    Pick a listed challenge or propose your own idea with your supervisor's agreement.

  2. Meet your host team

    A staff contact from Estates, Facilities or Sustainability agrees scope, site access and data needs.

  3. Get data access

    Approved projects receive access to the estate data they need, under a data-use agreement.

  4. Share what you find

    Present recommendations to the host team and deposit your output in the open research repository.

How a community challenge becomes a project

  1. Tell us the problem

    Use the short form. You don't need to frame it as research; we'll help with that.

  2. We talk it through

    A member of the Living Lab team calls you within two weeks to agree what's realistic.

  3. A student takes it on

    Projects start in September or January and usually run for one or two terms.

  4. You get the results

    The student presents findings to you, and a plain-English summary is published.

Living Labs with Bristol communities

UWE is co-developing Living Lab projects with neighbourhoods across Bristol: skills in St Paul's, repair with Sparks, citizen science air pollution sensors in Easton, the heat network in Barton Hill, and food growing in Eastville. The Frome Valley Pollinator Pathway connects with the Bristol Beeline.
